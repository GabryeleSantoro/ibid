# Building Raised Beds

## Size and depth

A raised bed about 30 cm deep suits almost every vegetable, from salads to carrots and potatoes. Keep the width to 120 cm or less so you can reach the middle from either side without stepping on the soil. Length is up to you, but leave paths wide enough for a wheelbarrow between beds. Untreated larch, oak or recycled scaffold boards all last for years; avoid old railway sleepers, which can leak tar.

## Filling the bed

Fill the bed with a soil mix of topsoil and compost, roughly two parts good topsoil to one part well-rotted compost. Break up the ground underneath with a fork first so the roots can carry on down and water drains away. Top up each spring with a few centimetres of fresh compost, because the level sinks as the organic matter breaks down.

## Why bother

Raised beds drain well on heavy clay, and the soil stays loose because nobody walks on it. They are also easier on the back. Best of all, the soil warms faster in spring than open ground, which means earlier sowings of peas, radishes and lettuce, often two or three weeks ahead of the rest of the garden.
