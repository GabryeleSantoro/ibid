# Watching Meteor Showers

## The Perseids

The Perseids are the best-known meteor shower of the year. They peak around 12 August, when a dark site can show up to 100 meteors per hour in the hours before dawn. The meteors are dust left behind by comet Swift–Tuttle, burning up as the Earth passes through its trail at almost sixty kilometres per second. Warm August nights make the Perseids the easiest shower to watch.

## The Geminids

The Geminids in December are the richest shower of the year, often matching or beating the Perseids. They come from an asteroid, 3200 Phaethon, rather than a comet, and their meteors are slower and often brightly coloured. Wrap up warm: Geminid nights are long and cold, but the radiant in Gemini is high before midnight.

## How to watch

You need no equipment at all. Lie back on a reclining chair, face away from the Moon and from town lights, and look at a wide stretch of sky about halfway up. Give your eyes twenty minutes to adapt and stay out for at least an hour, because meteors come in bursts and lulls. Count them with a friend and compare totals.
