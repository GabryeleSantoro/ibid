# Dealing with Garden Pests

## Aphids and ladybirds

Aphids cluster on soft new shoots and the undersides of leaves, sucking sap and spreading viruses. Before reaching for a spray, look for ladybirds. A single ladybird larva can eat hundreds of aphids before it pupates, and adult ladybirds lay their eggs near aphid colonies precisely because the larvae will need food. Rub small clusters off by hand, or knock them off with a jet of water, and leave the rest for the predators. Hoverflies and lacewings help too.

## Slug traps

Slugs do most of their damage at night, especially after rain. Beer traps work well: sink a jar into the soil with the rim a little above the surface and half fill it with cheap beer. Slugs are drawn to the smell and drown. Empty the traps every couple of days. A torch-lit patrol after dark is even more effective. Copper tape around pots and a ring of grit around young plants also help.

## Companion planting

Companion planting with marigolds is an old trick that still earns its place. French marigolds planted between tomatoes and beans release a scent that confuses whitefly, and their flowers draw in hoverflies whose larvae eat aphids. Nasturtiums act as a sacrificial crop, pulling blackfly away from the beans.
