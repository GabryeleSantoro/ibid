# Riding Up Hills

## Steady cadence

The key to climbing is a steady cadence. Pick a gear that lets you turn the pedals smoothly at around 75 to 85 revolutions per minute rather than grinding a big gear slowly. Grinding tires the muscles quickly and strains the knees; spinning a lighter gear shifts the work to your heart and lungs, which recover faster. Breathe deeply and rhythmically, and keep your upper body relaxed.

## Stay seated

On long climbs, stay seated for most of the way. Sitting down uses less energy than standing because your weight is supported by the saddle and your big leg muscles work efficiently. Stand up briefly to stretch, to accelerate over a steep ramp, or to change muscle groups for a minute, then sit back down. Slide back on the saddle a little to use your glutes.

## Low gears early

Shift into low gears early, before the gradient bites. Changing gear under heavy load on a steep slope makes the chain jump and can drop it entirely. Look ahead, read the road, and move to an easier gear a few metres before the slope steepens. Pace yourself on the first third of the climb so you have something left for the top.
