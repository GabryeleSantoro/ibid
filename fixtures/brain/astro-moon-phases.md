# Observing the Moon Through Its Phases

## The cycle

The Moon goes from new to full and back again in a 29.5-day cycle, the synodic month. At new moon it sits between the Earth and the Sun and we see only its dark side. As it moves along its orbit a growing crescent appears in the evening sky, reaching first quarter after about a week, full moon after two weeks, and last quarter after three.

## Watch the terminator

The terminator is the line between lunar day and night. Craters and mountains show best along the terminator, where the Sun is low on the lunar horizon and long shadows throw every rim and peak into relief. A crater that looks flat and washed out at full moon can look dramatic a few days later when the terminator passes it. For the most rewarding views, observe between the crescent and the gibbous phases and follow the line night after night.

## Full moon and faint objects

A full moon is beautiful but it washes out faint objects. Its light scatters across the whole sky and drowns galaxies, nebulae and the Milky Way. Plan deep-sky sessions for the week around new moon, and save the full moon for the bright planets and double stars.
