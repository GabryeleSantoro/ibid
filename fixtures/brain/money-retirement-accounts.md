# Saving for Retirement

## Start early

The best time to start saving for retirement is as early as possible. Money invested in your twenties has four decades to grow, and that time does more work than any later contribution can. Someone who saves for ten years from age 25 and then stops can end up with more than someone who saves every year from 35 to 65.

## Employer match

If your employer offers a pension match, take all of it. An employer match is free money: if they add one pound for every pound you put in, up to 5% of salary, then not contributing 5% is turning down a pay rise. Contributions often come with tax relief as well, so the true cost to you is lower still. Check the scheme's default fund and fees, and choose a low-cost global tracker if one is offered.

## Compound growth

Compound growth means your returns earn returns of their own. At 6% a year, money roughly doubles every twelve years. A pot that grows untouched for forty years can double more than three times. That is why withdrawing early or pausing contributions in your thirties is so costly: you lose not only the money but all the growth it would have earned for decades.
