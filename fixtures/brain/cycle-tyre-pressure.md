# Tyre Pressure for Road Bikes

## A starting point

Tyre pressure depends on tyre width and rider weight. As a starting point, 25 mm road tyres run around 80 psi for a 75 kg rider, a little more on the rear wheel, which carries most of the weight. Heavier riders add a few psi; lighter riders take some off. Wider tyres need less: 28 mm tyres for the same rider sit nearer 70 psi, and 32 mm tyres around 60 psi.

## Why lower can be faster

The old habit of pumping road tyres to 120 psi made the ride harsh and, on real roads, slower. A very hard tyre bounces over every ripple instead of absorbing it, wasting energy and grip. Tubeless tyres run lower still, because there is no inner tube to pinch against the rim on a pothole. Lower pressure gives more grip in corners and in the wet, and far more comfort on long rides.

## Check weekly

Tyres lose pressure slowly even without a puncture, and inner tubes made of latex lose it overnight. Check weekly with a track pump that has a proper gauge, ideally before every long ride. Write your front and rear pressures on a piece of tape on the pump so you get them the same every time.
