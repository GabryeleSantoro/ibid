# Mortgage Basics

## Fixed or variable rate

A fixed rate mortgage keeps the same interest rate for an agreed period, typically two, five or ten years, so the monthly payment does not change. A variable rate follows the lender's rate or a central-bank base rate, so payments can rise or fall. Fixed rates give certainty for budgeting; variable rates are often cheaper at the start but carry the risk of sharp rises. When a fixed deal ends, the loan usually moves to an expensive standard variable rate, so diarise the date and remortgage in time.

## Deposit size

The deposit size changes everything. Lenders price mortgages by loan-to-value: a 10% deposit means a 90% loan-to-value, and the best rates usually start at 60% or 75%. A bigger deposit lowers the rate, shrinks the loan and protects you if house prices fall. Saving an extra year to move from a 5% to a 10% deposit can save thousands.

## Total interest over the term

Look beyond the monthly payment at the total interest over the term. Stretching a loan from 25 to 35 years lowers the payment but can add tens of thousands in interest. Overpaying a little each month, where the lender allows it, shortens the term and cuts that total.
