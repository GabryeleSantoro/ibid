# Building an Emergency Fund

## How much

An emergency fund is money set aside for the unexpected: losing a job, a broken boiler, a sudden vet bill. The usual guidance is to save three to six months of expenses. Count only essential spending for this: rent or mortgage, utilities, food, insurance, transport and minimum debt payments. Someone with a steady salary and no dependants can lean towards three months; a freelancer, a single-income household or anyone in a volatile industry should aim nearer six.

## Where to keep it

Keep it in an easy-access savings account, separate from your everyday current account so it is not spent by accident. The point is that the money is there immediately and its value does not move. Do not invest the emergency fund in shares: a market crash and a job loss often arrive together, and you do not want to sell at the bottom. Shop around for the best easy-access interest rate, but never lock it into a fixed-term deposit.

## Building it up

Start small. Even a first goal of one month's expenses changes how a crisis feels. Set up an automatic transfer on payday, and send windfalls such as tax refunds straight to the fund. Once it is full, redirect the transfer to investing.
