# Choosing a First Telescope

## Aperture over magnification

The most important number on a telescope is its aperture: the diameter of the main lens or mirror. Aperture matters more than magnification because it sets how much light the telescope gathers. A larger mirror collects more light, so faint galaxies and nebulae become visible and fine detail on the planets sharpens. Magnification only enlarges whatever image the aperture delivers. Push it too far and you get a big, dim, blurry view. Boxes that shout "600x power" are selling the wrong thing; useful magnification tops out around twice the aperture in millimetres.

## The 200 mm Dobsonian

For most beginners the best first scope is a 200 mm Dobsonian. It is a simple Newtonian reflector on a wooden rocker box that you push by hand, with no motors and no batteries to fail on a cold night. Eight inches of aperture shows the rings of Saturn, the cloud bands of Jupiter, hundreds of deep-sky objects and craters down to a few kilometres on the Moon, at a price that would buy a much smaller scope on a computerised mount.

## Things to check

Make sure it comes with a red-dot finder and a low-power eyepiece. Let the mirror cool outside for half an hour before observing.
