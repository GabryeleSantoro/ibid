# Making Stock

## Roast the bones

For a deep brown stock, roast the bones first. Spread chicken carcasses or beef bones on a tray with chopped onion, carrot and celery and roast them at 220 °C for about forty minutes, until well browned. The browning adds colour and a rich, savoury flavour. Deglaze the tray with a splash of water and scrape every bit into the pot. For a light stock for soups and risotto, skip the roasting and start from raw bones.

## Simmer, never boil

Cover the bones with cold water and bring them up slowly. Simmer, never boil. A rolling boil emulsifies the fat into the liquid and turns the stock cloudy and greasy. Aim for a bare tremble, with a bubble breaking the surface every second or two. Chicken stock needs three to four hours; beef stock can go for six or more. Add herbs such as parsley stalks, bay and thyme towards the end.

## Skim the foam

In the first half hour, grey foam rises to the surface. Skim the foam off with a ladle or a large spoon as it appears; it is mostly protein and fat and leaves a muddy taste. When the stock is done, strain it through a fine sieve, chill it, and lift off the fat that sets on top.
