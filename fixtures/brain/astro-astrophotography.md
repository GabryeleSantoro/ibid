# Starting Astrophotography

## The 500 rule

With a camera on a fixed tripod, stars start to trail if the exposure is too long, because the Earth is turning. The 500 rule gives a quick limit: divide 500 by the focal length of the lens to get the longest exposure in seconds before trails become visible. A 20 mm lens allows about 25 seconds; a 50 mm lens only 10. On cameras with a cropped sensor, multiply the focal length by the crop factor first.

## Tracking mounts

To go past the 500 rule you need a tracking mount. A star tracker is a small motorised head that turns the camera at the same rate as the sky, once polar-aligned on the celestial pole. With good alignment, exposures of several minutes at long focal lengths become possible, and faint nebulae begin to show colour.

## Stacking frames

A single long exposure is noisy. Stacking frames solves this: take dozens of identical exposures of the same target, plus dark frames with the lens cap on, and combine them in software such as DeepSkyStacker or Siril. Random noise averages out while the faint signal adds up, so the final image is far cleaner than any single shot.
