# The 50/30/20 Budget

## The rule

The 50/30/20 rule is a simple way to split take-home pay into three parts. 50% goes to needs: rent or mortgage, utilities, groceries, insurance, transport to work and minimum debt payments. 30% goes to wants: eating out, holidays, subscriptions, hobbies and new clothes that are not strictly necessary. The last 20% goes to savings and debt repayment: building the emergency fund, investing for retirement, and paying more than the minimum on any loans or cards.

## Making it work

Start by tracking a couple of months of spending and sorting each item into one of the three groups. Most people find their needs are well above half, usually because of housing costs. If so, treat the percentages as targets rather than rules and nudge them over time, for example by moving somewhere cheaper when a lease ends or refinancing a car loan.

## Why it helps

The rule is easy to remember and does not need a spreadsheet with forty categories. It also gives explicit permission to spend 30% on things you enjoy, which makes the budget easier to stick with. Automate the 20% on payday so saving happens before spending, not with whatever is left at the end of the month.
