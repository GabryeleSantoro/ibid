# Pruning Roses

## When to prune

Prune most bush and shrub roses in late winter, just as the buds begin to swell but before the leaves open. In a mild area that is usually late February; in colder places wait until early March so the fresh cuts are not damaged by a hard frost. Rambling roses are the exception: they flower on last year's wood and are pruned straight after flowering in summer.

## Dead wood first

Always start by removing dead wood. Dead stems are brown or grey and brittle, with no green under the bark when you scratch it. Next take out diseased stems and any that cross and rub against each other. Then thin the centre of the bush so air can move freely through it, which keeps black spot down.

## Making the cut

Use clean, sharp secateurs. Cut about five millimetres above an outward bud, at an angle of 45° sloping away from the bud. An outward-facing bud sends the new shoot away from the centre, giving an open, goblet-shaped plant. The sloping cut lets rain run off instead of sitting on the bud and rotting it. On hybrid teas, shorten the strongest stems to about a third of their length.
