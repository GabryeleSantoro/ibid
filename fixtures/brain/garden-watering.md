# Watering the Garden

## Deep and infrequent

Most vegetable beds and borders do best when you water deeply once or twice a week rather than lightly every day. A deep soak pushes moisture down 20 or 30 cm, and the roots grow down to follow it. Shallow daily watering keeps the roots near the surface, where they dry out and suffer as soon as you miss a day. Check by pushing a finger into the soil: if it is dry at the depth of your second knuckle, it is time to water.

## Morning is best

Water in the morning whenever you can. The plants have moisture to draw on during the heat of the day, and any splashes on the leaves dry quickly in the sun, which reduces mildew and other fungal problems. Evening watering leaves foliage damp all night and attracts slugs. Midday watering in full sun loses much of the water to evaporation before it reaches the roots.

## Mulch keeps soil moist

A layer of mulch five to eight centimetres thick keeps the soil moist far longer. Use compost, leaf mould, straw or bark chips, spread over damp soil and kept a little away from plant stems. Mulch shades the surface, slows evaporation, keeps weeds down and feeds the soil as it breaks down over the season.
