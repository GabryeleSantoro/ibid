# Basic Bike Fit

## Saddle height

Saddle height is the first and most important adjustment. With your heel on the pedal at the bottom of the stroke, your leg should be straight; when you clip in with the ball of the foot, that leaves a slight knee bend of around 25 to 35 degrees. Too low and the knees take the strain and you lose power. Too high and the hips rock side to side on every pedal stroke. Change it in small steps of five millimetres.

## Reach

Reach is the distance from the saddle to the handlebars. You should be able to hold the hoods with a slight bend in the elbows and relaxed shoulders, without stretching or locking your arms. If your hands go numb or your neck aches, the reach is probably too long or the bars too low. A shorter stem, or moving the spacers, is the usual fix.

## Cleat position

Cleat position sets how the foot sits on the pedal. Place the cleat so the ball of the foot sits over the pedal axle, and set the angle to match how your feet naturally point when you walk. Knee pain on the inside or outside of the joint often means the cleat angle needs changing.
