# Basic Knife Skills

## The claw grip

The claw grip keeps your fingers safe. Curl the fingertips of the hand holding the food under, so the knuckles face the blade and the tips are tucked behind them. The flat of the knife rests against the knuckles, which guide each cut, and the fingertips are never in the way. Move the claw back a little after each slice. It feels slow at first, but with practice it becomes faster than any other way of holding food.

## Keep the knife sharp

Keep the knife sharp. A sharp knife is safer than a blunt one, because it bites into the food instead of slipping off a tomato skin or an onion and into your hand. Hone the edge with a steel before each session to straighten it, and sharpen on a whetstone every few months to restore it. Never put good knives in the dishwasher, and store them in a block or on a magnetic strip.

## Rock chop for herbs

For herbs and garlic, use the rock chop. Keep the tip of a chef's knife on the board, rest the other hand flat on the spine near the tip, and rock the handle up and down while sweeping the blade across the pile. Gather the herbs back into a heap and repeat until they are as fine as you want.
