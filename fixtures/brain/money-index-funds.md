# Why Index Funds

## Low fees

An index fund simply buys every company in a market index, such as a global share index, in proportion to its size. There is no team of analysts picking stocks, so the fees are very low, often well under 0.2% a year against 1% or more for actively managed funds. That difference sounds small, but over thirty years the extra fee compounds and can take a fifth of your final pot. Most active funds fail to beat their index after fees anyway.

## Diversification

Buying the whole market gives diversification across the market in a single purchase. Your money is spread over thousands of companies in many countries and sectors, so one firm collapsing barely registers. A global tracker removes the need to guess which country or industry will do best next decade.

## Time in the market

Time in the market beats timing the market. Nobody reliably predicts crashes or rallies, and missing just the ten best days in a decade, which often come right after the worst ones, cuts returns sharply. Invest regularly, for example monthly, and leave it alone. Rebalance once a year if you also hold bonds, and resist the urge to sell when headlines turn gloomy.
