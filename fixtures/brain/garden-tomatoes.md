# Growing Tomatoes

## Staking and side shoots

Cordon tomatoes grow as a single tall stem, so each plant needs a sturdy stake or a string tied to an overhead wire from the day it goes in. Tie the main stem loosely every 30 cm as it climbs, using soft twine that will not cut into the skin. Every week, look in the angle between each leaf and the main stem: the small shoot growing there is a side shoot. Pinch side shoots out with finger and thumb while they are still short. Left alone they turn into extra stems that crowd the plant, shade the fruit and take energy away from the trusses that are already setting.

## Watering at the base

Water tomatoes at the base of the plant, never over the leaves. Wet foliage invites blight and other fungal diseases, especially in a humid greenhouse. A deep soak every few days is better than a light sprinkle every evening, because the roots follow the water downwards.

## Blossom-end rot

A dark, sunken patch at the bottom of the fruit is blossom-end rot. It is caused by uneven watering: when the soil swings between bone dry and soaked, the plant cannot move enough calcium into the developing fruit. Keep the moisture steady, mulch the pots, and never let grow bags dry out completely in a heatwave.
