# Planets to See from the Back Garden

## Jupiter and its moons

Jupiter is the easiest planet to enjoy. Even steady binoculars show its four Galilean moons: Io, Europa, Ganymede and Callisto, lined up like beads on either side of the disc. Their positions change from night to night, and sometimes from hour to hour, as they orbit. In a small telescope the two dark equatorial belts are clear, and on good nights the Great Red Spot comes into view.

## Saturn's rings

Saturn's rings are visible in a small scope, even a 60 mm refractor at about 40x. The first sight of them is often the moment people get hooked on astronomy. A larger aperture shows the Cassini division, a dark gap in the rings, and the moon Titan nearby. The tilt of the rings changes over the years; every fifteen years they turn edge-on and almost vanish.

## Venus phases

Venus shows phases like the Moon, because it orbits closer to the Sun than we do. As it swings towards us it grows larger but thinner, from a small gibbous disc to a big thin crescent. Galileo's observations of Venus's phases were evidence that it goes around the Sun. Observe it in twilight, when its glare is lower.
