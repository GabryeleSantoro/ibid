# Getting the Most from Spices

## Toast whole spices

Buy whole spices where you can: cumin, coriander, fennel seeds, cardamom, black pepper. Before using them, toast whole spices in a dry pan over a medium heat, shaking the pan, for a minute or two until they smell fragrant and darken slightly. Heat releases the aromatic oils and transforms the flavour. Watch closely, because seeds go from toasted to burnt in seconds; tip them onto a cold plate as soon as they are done.

## Grind fresh

Grind fresh, just before cooking. Ground spice loses its aroma within weeks because the oils escape from all that exposed surface. A cheap electric coffee grinder kept only for spices, or a pestle and mortar, does the job. Grinding a teaspoon of cumin takes thirty seconds and tastes far brighter than any jar from the supermarket.

## Storage

Store spices away from light and heat, in airtight jars in a drawer or cupboard rather than in a rack above the cooker. Light fades both the colour and the flavour, and steam from the hob makes ground spices clump. Whole spices keep for a year or two; ground spices are best used within six months. Write the purchase date on each jar.
