# Commuting by Bike

## Lights and visibility

For a year-round commute, lights and visibility come first. Fit a bright white front light and a red rear light, and use them in the day as well as at night: daytime flashing modes make you easier to spot among parked cars. Add reflective details on the pedals, ankles and bags, because movement catches drivers' eyes. Position yourself where drivers expect traffic, away from the door zone of parked cars.

## Panniers

Panniers beat a backpack on any commute longer than a couple of kilometres. They carry a laptop, clothes and shopping low on the bike, which keeps the centre of gravity down, and your back does not end up soaked with sweat. Waterproof roll-top panniers clip on and off the rack in seconds and go straight to the desk.

## Mudguards

Mudguards are the most underrated accessory. Without them, a wet road sprays a stripe of dirty water up your back and over your shoes, and onto whoever rides behind you. Full-length mudguards with a rubber flap keep you and the bike far cleaner and mean the chain and brakes need less cleaning. Most commuter frames have mounts for them; clip-on versions fit almost any bike.
