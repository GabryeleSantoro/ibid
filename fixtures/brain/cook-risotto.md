# Making Risotto

## Toast the rice

Use a short-grain risotto rice such as Arborio or Carnaroli. Soften a finely chopped onion in butter and olive oil over a gentle heat, then add the rice and toast the rice for two or three minutes, stirring, until the grains turn translucent at the edges and smell nutty. Toasting seals the outside of each grain so it releases its starch slowly and keeps a little bite. Pour in a glass of dry white wine and let it evaporate completely.

## A ladle at a time

Keep a pan of stock simmering on the next ring. Add hot stock a ladle at a time, stirring often, and wait until each ladle is almost absorbed before adding the next. Cold stock stops the cooking and makes the rice gluey. The stirring rubs starch from the grains, which is what makes risotto creamy without any cream. After about eighteen minutes the rice should be tender but still firm in the centre, and loose enough to flow.

## Finish with butter and parmesan

Take the pan off the heat and finish with butter and parmesan, beating them in hard for a minute. Italians call this the mantecatura. Cover, rest for two minutes, then serve on warm plates.
