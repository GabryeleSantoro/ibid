# Making Compost

## Browns and greens

A healthy compost heap balances carbon and nitrogen. Aim for a carbon-to-nitrogen ratio of about 30:1. In practice that means mixing "browns", which are rich in carbon, with "greens", which are rich in nitrogen. Browns include dry leaves, shredded cardboard, straw and woody prunings. Greens include grass clippings, vegetable peelings, coffee grounds and fresh weeds before they set seed. Too many greens and the heap turns slimy and smells of ammonia; too many browns and it sits there for a year doing nothing. A rough rule is two or three buckets of browns for every bucket of greens.

## Turning the heap

Microbes that break down the material need air as well as food. Turn the heap every two weeks with a fork, moving the dry outside layer into the warm middle. Each turn brings a new burst of heat, and a well-built heap can reach 60 °C in the centre. If it is dry, add water until it feels like a wrung-out sponge.

## When is it ready

Turned regularly, compost is ready in 3–6 months. Finished compost is dark, crumbly and smells of a forest floor. You should no longer recognise the original peelings. Sieve out anything woody and throw it back on the next heap to start again.
